Water hammer →

Shut a valve on a moving column of water and the pressure spike that follows is set by how fast you shut it — not by how hard the pump was pushing. Close it yourself, fast or slow, and watch the wave run the pipe.

Cavitation →

One pump, one pipe, one duty — and the only thing you change is how hot the liquid is. The pressure arriving at the pump barely moves. What moves is the pressure at which the liquid boils, and it climbs until it reaches the pump.

  • zero network requests

Thermal runaway →

Warm a lithium cell's surroundings degree by degree. For a long way it just sits there, a little above ambient, looking like a healthy cell — and then one degree removes the temperature it was settling at, and there is nothing left to settle at.

Restrained expansion →

Hold steel at both ends and warm it, and the stress does not depend on how long it is — six metres and a hundred and twenty reach the same number. Allow a few millimetres of movement and suddenly length is the only thing that matters.

Stress concentration →

A round hole triples the stress beside it — at any size, pinhole or doorway. Flatten the same hole into a slit and the multiplier runs away, while the material you removed accounts for almost none of it.

Creep →

Ten degrees hotter halves how long it lasts; fifty takes a decade down to a season. Creep trades time against temperature at a savage exchange rate — and leaves nothing to inspect for until the end.

Resonance →

The instinct is to stiffen it. Stiffening moves the resonance somewhere else and leaves the peak exactly as tall — the height is set by damping and nothing else. And away from resonance, damping does almost nothing.

If one of these is your failure mode — a mechanism or a process people keep getting wrong because the diagram cannot move — I build them to order, from £4,000, and they take three to four weeks. yinxueshi1017@gmail.com

And if one of them is wrong — a mechanism left out, numbers that do not match what you see in the field — tell me in one line. Corrections are the main thing that decides what gets built next.

The physics in these pieces is checked against published standards — Eurocode, Barsom, Inglis, Semenov, Joukowsky, and handbook tables for water — by a script you can run yourself: audit.py in the source. It reads the constants out of these pages and compares each claim against a value from somewhere else, so a mistake shared between a model and its own check cannot hide. It has not been reviewed by an independent engineer, and each piece says so on its own page.
