This is an interactive demonstration of hydrogen embrittlement, and it needs JavaScript to run. What it shows: a high-strength bolt can absorb hydrogen during plating, and under sustained load that hydrogen gathers at the first engaged thread — completely invisibly, with the bolt passing any visual inspection — until the bolt breaks without warning. Resting the part does undo the concentration, but far too slowly to be any use in production, which is why suspect parts are baked in an oven instead. Nothing about the part's appearance ever changes before the break: that is the point, and it is why this page is a demonstration rather than a picture.

What is not verified

Stated plainly, because a piece whose value is trustworthiness should not overclaim.

  • No engineer has reviewed the physics. The proportions were checked against ISO metric coarse practice — ISO 68-1, 4014, 4032, 7089/7092 and 273 — and against ASTM B850 and F1940 for baking practice, and the joint mechanics hold up. But that check was the author's own. Before this is relied on as authority, it wants a metallurgist's eye.
  • The sound path is measured on one browser engine (Chromium). The audio is opt-in and the piece is complete without it.
  • The frame budget is measured in a software rasteriser, which is slower than the integrated graphics this targets — so those numbers are a floor, not a forecast.
