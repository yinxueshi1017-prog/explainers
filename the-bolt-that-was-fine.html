The whole argument is that one panel never changes

Hydrogen collects at the first engaged thread root under load. It is invisible from outside. The failure gives no warning, which is exactly why suspect parts get baked in an oven rather than inspected.

So the piece makes a promise and then keeps it mechanically: whatever the hydrogen is doing, the exterior panel is pixel-identical. That is not a claim in the copy. It is an assertion in the test suite, and it fails the build if it ever stops being true.

Method

Not a linear build. A loop, run until the picture stopped getting better — and the numbering here is the real sequence, not decoration.

01

Render it and look at it honestly

At the size it will actually be embedded, on an ordinary screen. Five separate "defects" this project chased turned out to exist only at 3–4× zoom.

02

Measure the thing you think is wrong

Before touching it. Roughly half the time the measurement refuted the complaint and the fix was never written.

03

Fix the single worst thing

One change, one reason, one commit message that records why — including the alternatives that were built, measured and rejected.

04

Prove it with an instrument that can fail

Every negative control in the suite is itself tested: the watertightness check punches a deliberate hole and asserts that it sees it.

05

Run the whole gate, then commit

Seven suites. A change that improves the picture and breaks the physics is not an improvement.

Five things I got wrong

Every portfolio shows the finished thing. Here is the part that actually tells you whether the engineering is honest — each of these shipped wrong, and each was caught by an instrument rather than by an opinion.

The loaded bolt was drawn already severed

Elastic stretch was applied as a rigid translation, which opened a void exactly at the plane where the crack would later run. The piece's entire argument is that the failure gives no warning — and the picture was previewing it on every loaded frame.

Caught by walking a line down the section and asking the DOM what bounded it: black-run columns went 9 → 78 as load rose.

A note in my own file said not to look here

A comment recorded that splitting the plate's side faces had been measured worse. It had — by an instrument of mine that counted correct occlusion as error and used a tolerance five hundred times too loose. Three independent audits disagreed with it and I sided with my own bad number.

Caught by ray-casting true depth at the contested pixel: the plate is at 1.545, the washer painted over it at 1.707.

The seam stroke was specified in the wrong units

Every polygon strokes itself to close the antialiasing gap at shared edges. The width was in model units, so at real embed size it rendered 0.43 of a pixel and closed less than half the gap. The bolt head read as a fluted drum.

Caught by judging at ordinary pixel density — at 2× the defect is nearly absent, which is how it survived so long.

The node pool was silently dropping the front of the picture

The SVG node pool stops at its end, and the primitive list is sorted back to front — so overflowing discarded the nearest geometry, in silence. The comment beside it named that exact risk and had gone stale as the model gained detail.

Caught by a sweep that cracks the bolt at its most expensive angle: demand peaks at the crack moment, not at rest.

Breaking the bolt turned the surviving thread to chalk

At fracture the whole lower half swapped to the crystalline material so the crack face could shine — taking the exposed thread stub, still machined steel, with it. The payoff frame was the one that looked worst.

Caught by an exact geometric mask: the stub's highlight tail collapsed from p90 79 to 45 and its relief halved.

What it does when it breaks

There is no countdown and no warning colour. The break is sudden because the failure is sudden, and the recovery afterwards is deliberately slow and partial — a rested part is not a safe part.

>8×full recovery extrapolates to over eightfold the charging time

What is not verified

Stated plainly, because a piece whose value is trustworthiness should not overclaim.

  • No human engineer has reviewed the physics. Proportions were checked against ISO metric coarse practice and the joint mechanics hold up, but that check was mine. Before this ships as authority, it wants a metallurgist's eye.
  • The sound path is measured on one browser engine. Chromium here; the audio is opt-in and the piece is complete without it.
  • Frame budget is measured in a software rasteriser, which is slower than the integrated graphics it targets — so the numbers are a floor, not a forecast.
